# Lifecycle Activation Playbook

This is a proposed operating plan based on historical RFM segments, not evidence that messages were sent or that they improved sales. The dashboard contains illustrative email and 15-second video examples for every segment.

| Segment | Observed audience | Primary job | First test | Success metric |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| Champions | 1,292 | Protect loyalty and service | Early access vs. regular editorial message | Incremental repeat revenue per assigned customer |
| At Risk | 825 | Win back relevant customers | Curated reminder vs. capped incentive | 30-day reactivation; revenue after discount |
| Hibernating | 1,527 | Re-establish interest or suppress | Preference refresh vs. no contact | Re-permission rate; complaint and unsubscribe rate |
| New Customers | 167 | Build first-90-day habit | Usage tip vs. product pairing | Second purchase within 90 days |

Counts are a single historical RFM snapshot, not a live CRM audience. At Risk and Hibernating total 2,352 in that snapshot. The A/B plan's 1,293-person historical eligible pool uses a different time-anchored inactivity rule; do not treat these counts as interchangeable.

## Content production workflow

1. Export only aggregate segment facts for planning. Do not paste customer IDs, purchase histories, email addresses, or other personal data into a generative model.
2. Draft a channel brief: audience, benefit, one claim supported by the product catalog, desired action, offer terms, exclusions, and success metric.
3. Use AI, if available, to propose **variants**, not final publishable copy. Example prompt: “Draft three concise email subject/body and three 15-second vertical-video scripts for a consented At Risk retail audience. Use a calm, helpful tone. Do not invent discounts, scarcity, product benefits, testimonials, or personalization facts. Mark any claim that needs verification.”
4. Human-review facts, brand voice, accessibility, consent and suppression rules, discount economics, and mobile rendering. Log approved version IDs before scheduling.
5. Randomize at customer level where feasible; keep control/treatment content and offer differences explicit. Report delivered, open/view, click, 30-day reactivation, and revenue per assigned customer, but do not substitute opens or views for business impact.

## Example creative brief: At Risk

- **Audience:** consented customers fitting the experiment's 60–180-day inactivity rule at a fixed anchor date.
- **Hypothesis:** a helpful curated reminder increases return visits; a capped offer may increase reactivation but can reduce value after discount.
- **Email A:** editorial reminder, no discount. Subject: “A few ideas for your next visit”. Body: “Explore a small edit of products chosen around common shopping needs. Take a look whenever it suits you.”
- **Email B:** same message plus a separately approved, clearly stated offer. Do not draft or send until real offer terms and exclusions exist.
- **Video:** 0–4s familiar shopping need; 4–10s two products in use; 10–15s one call to action. Caption product details and offer terms. Use owned or licensed footage only.
- **Guardrails:** consent and unsubscribe compliance, complaints, discount cost, revenue per assigned customer, and contribution margin when cost data becomes available.

## Portfolio presentation evidence

Use the dashboard's segment view and scenario calculator as screenshots. Label the charts “historical analysis” and the messages “proposed creative.” The most defensible story is the chain from observed segment behavior to a testable campaign brief, not a claim of generated sales or launched automation.
